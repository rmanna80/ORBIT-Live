      - Understanding gravity and why physicists use μ = GM
        - Newton discovered that any two objects with mass attract each other.
          - Earth attracts the Moon.
          - The Sun attracts Earth.
          - Earth attracts a satellite.
          - Even two people standing beside each other attract gravitationally, although the force is tiny.
            - F = GMm/r^2
              - F = Gravitational force, measured in newtons
              - G = Universal gravitational constant
              - M = Mass of Earth (or another central body)
              - m = Mass of the satellite
              - r = Distance between the centers of the two objects
                - This tell us 2 key detials:
                  - More mass means more gravitational attraction
                  - Greater distance means weaker gravitational attraction
                    - Because distance is squared, doubling the distance reduces gravitational force to one-quarter of its original strength.
                      - another Newton Equation: F = m * a 
                        - Force = mass * acceleration
                          - acceleration = how quickly velocity changes
                            - A satellite can be accelerating even when its speed stays constant, because its direction is changing
              - when you combine the two Equations the small m cancels out
                - from that we discover:  Under the same conditions, a satellite's gravitational acceleration does not depend on how much the satellite weighs.
                  - A 100 kg satellite and a 1,000 kg satellite follow the same ideal orbit when starting with identical positions and velocities.

      - What is μ?
        - standard gravitational parameter
          - μ describes the strength of a planet's gravity in a form that's directly useful for orbital calculations

- Kepler's third law — How long does a satellite take to orbit Earth?
  - The farther out the orbit is, the longer the satellite takes to complete one revolution
    - First law: Orbits are ellipses:
      - Planets generally follow oval-shaped paths, with the Sun at one focus rather than necessarily at the center.
    - Second Law; Orbital speed changes 
      - A satellite moves faster when closer to the object it orbits and slower when farther away. The line connecting them sweeps out equal areas in equal times
    - Third Law: Bigger orbits take longer
      - The orbital period squared is proportional to the orbit's semi-major axis cubed

- Kepler's equation — Predicting where the satellite will be
  - First, understand three different angles (all describe progress around an elliptical orbit)
    - Angle:                Symbol:              Meaning:
      Mean anomaly           M                   How far along the orbit we'd be if angular motion were uniform
      Eccentric anomaly      E                   A mathematical helper angle based on a circle surrounding the ellipse
      True anomaly           v                   The actual angle locating the satellite relative to Earth

      - Think of mean anomaly as a clock measuring progress around the orbit.

      - But in an elliptical orbit, the satellite's true anomaly generally differs from its mean anomaly because its speed varies.
